How should I store and label loose LEGO® parts?

Storage works when you can find a part without opening every box. For a collection too big for a few boxes, the same rules scale.

The answer

  1. 1
    Sort by part type, not by colourFinding a red 1×2 plate among all the 1×2 plates is faster than finding it among all the red parts.
  2. 2
    Give every place a nameRoom, shelf, drawer, bin: "Shelf A, Drawer 3" beats "the grey box".
  3. 3
    Nest the placesA bin sits in a drawer, the drawer on a shelf. Then a short name is enough.
  4. 4
    Keep a list of what is whereIncluding sets you stored taken apart, each in its own bag or box.
  5. 5
    Label lastOnce the places have settled. A label that matches your list is the one you trust.
Labels are not in the demoThe demo shows a sample collection filed in its storages. The tutorial shows how to print the labels.

How Brick Work does it

In Storage, every place is a storage you name: a box, shelf, drawer, bin, bag, case, cabinet or room. Storages can sit inside each other.

  • Give a set copy, a minifigure or a part lot its place, one at a time or many at once. Anything without a place shows under Not stored.
  • Open a storage to see what it holds, grouped as sets, minifigures and part lots, with search and filters.
  • Print labels makes QR bin labels on A4 sheets (21 or 14 per sheet) or 62 mm label tape. Each label carries a short id that is never reused. Scan it with your phone camera to open that bin's contents. These are bin labels with a QR code, not labels with part pictures. How to print the labels.
  • Every move is listed in the storage's History, with Undo.
