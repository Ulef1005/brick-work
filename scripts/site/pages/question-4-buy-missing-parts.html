Missing LEGO® parts: Pick a Brick or BrickLink?

The cheapest part is the one you already own. After that, a single list beats many small orders.

The answer

  1. 1
    Check your own loose parts firstA spare from another set or a bulk lot costs nothing.
  2. 2
    BatchPut the missing parts of every set you want to finish into one list before you buy anything. Orders and shipping add up per order, not per part.
  3. 3
    Split the list by source, line by lineThe LEGO Group's Pick a Brick sells new single parts by their element number. Its range changes through the year, and parts can be unavailable for months, so check what it carries in your country. BrickLink is a marketplace of sellers, with new and used parts and older parts that are no longer made.
  4. 4
    Compare per line, not per shopNeither source is cheaper for everything.
In the demoOrder missing parts lists what four incomplete sets are missing. The guide walks through the export.

How Brick Work does it

Order missing parts turns the gaps you recorded into one order list.

  • Tick the sets you want to finish. Sets tagged to sell or part out are left out by default.
  • Each line is one part, colour and condition, combined over the ticked sets. A chip names the set it comes from, such as 6080-1 ×2.
  • Turn on Use loose stock in the repair settings, and your loose parts are taken off first. It is off until you turn it on.
  • Each line goes to BrickLink, with its stored price and its age, or to Pick a Brick when Brick Work holds the part's element number. You choose. Brick Work does not ask the LEGO Group for prices, so it cannot pick the cheaper source for you.
  • Download a BrickLink wanted list (XML) and a Pick a Brick file (CSV) to upload on each site.
  • Before you buy, Part Runner leads you through your drawers to pull what you already hold.

The page only reads and changes nothing in your collection.
