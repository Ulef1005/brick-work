Which LEGO® sets could my bulk lot be from?

No photo of a pile tells you its sets. The parts that only a few sets hold do.

The answer

  1. 1
    Pick out the evidenceMinifigures, printed parts and unusual parts in unusual colours. A plain 2×4 brick is in thousands of sets and says almost nothing.
  2. 2
    Look each one upIn an online parts catalogue, to see which sets hold it.
  3. 3
    Note the sets that come up again and againThey are the sets the lot may have come from.
  4. 4
    Check the strongest candidateTake its parts list and check it against the pile.
  5. 5
    Expect gapsA bulk lot is often parts of many sets, and rarely one set complete.
Not in the demoLot Sorter needs a lot you entered. The tutorial shows every step with screenshots.

How Brick Work does it

In Brick Work, a lot is a pile of loose parts you record. You enter it part by part, with Part Spotter and your camera or by hand. Then Lot Sorter's set detection answers Which sets are in this lot? with the sets the lot may have come from, ranked by score.

  • The score weighs the parts that point to a set: distinctive parts (held by 25 sets or fewer), printed parts only that set holds, and how many of its part lines the lot has. Parts that more than 300 sets hold are left out by default.
  • The best candidate scores 100 %, and the others are measured against it. It is evidence, not a probability. Each candidate says why, such as "20 of 133 distinctive parts found".
  • Details shows the distinctive parts found and the ones still to dig for. Narrow the list by score, theme, years and size.
  • A part with a sticker can be pinned to the set whose sticker you recognise. A confirmed sticker counts strongly.
  • Open a candidate's Pull list and tick the parts as you dig them out. Make it a copy moves them into a new copy of the set and records what you did not find as missing. One Undo takes it back.

Set detection only reads and changes nothing in your collection.
