What is my incomplete LEGO® set worth?

An incomplete set is worth what a complete used one sells for, less what it would cost a buyer to finish it. Its parts sold one by one set the lowest price that makes sense.

The answer

  1. 1
    Look up recent used salesUse sold prices, not asking prices.
  2. 2
    List what is missingEach part and each minifigure.
  3. 3
    Take off each missing pieceAt what it costs to buy used. Minifigures often make the biggest difference.
  4. 4
    Work out the part-out valueWhat all the parts you have would fetch sold one at a time.
  5. 5
    Compare the twoIf the parts are worth clearly more than the set, parting out may pay, if the work and fees are worth it to you. If the figures are close, selling the set whole is less work.
  6. 6
    Say exactly what is missingIn the listing. Buyers trust a listing that says exactly what is missing.
Needs a BrickLink keyOn your own install. The demo uses saved prices, so it needs none.

How Brick Work does it

On a set's page, Brick Work puts the figures side by side for the copy you hold.

  • Prices: BrickLink's used and new sold average for the set, with the number of sales and the age of the quote.
  • Parted out: what the copy's parts and figures fetch one at a time, shown only when at least 90 % of the lines have a price. When the parts clearly beat the copy's value, a line says by how much.
  • Sell this copy: an asking price that starts from the used sold average and takes off the used price of each missing part and figure, line by line. A line with no fresh price is marked and left out of the sum. The part-out value is the floor.
  • Preview writes a listing text that names the set, its condition, what is missing and whether the box and instructions are there. You can edit and copy it. Brick Work posts nothing.

The prices come from BrickLink, so your own install needs a BrickLink key. Without one you only see prices already stored. The deductions are exact once you have checked the copy part by part.
