# Identify a loose piece and find where it belongs

You hold a loose minifigure or part and do not know where it came from. In this tutorial you photograph a figure, open its page and follow one of its parts to the sets that contain it. Then you do the same with a loose part and end on a set you own.

It takes about 10 minutes. You need a Brick Work install with the catalogue loaded, a camera or a saved photo, and one loose figure or part to hold up. Identifying uses [Brickognize](../how-to/identify-a-minifigure.md), an outside service, and one photo costs one request from the daily budget. Every picture comes from an invented collection, so your own numbers and names will differ.

## 1. Identify the figure

1. Open the jump search (**Jump to a set, copy, figure or part**, or ⌘K) and choose **What is this? · identify a part by photo**.
2. Under **Recognise**, choose **Minifigure**.
3. Press **Enable camera** and allow camera access. Place the figure inside the guide and press **Take picture**. Or press **Choose a saved photo**.
4. Read the cards. They list the best match first, with its number, a match score from 0 to 1 and what you already have of it, or **Not in your collection**.

The page saves nothing. If the list says **Nothing identified · try another photo**, take the picture again against a plain background. The camera needs a secure page (HTTPS or `localhost`); elsewhere only the saved photo is offered.

## 2. Open the figure

1. On the card that shows your figure, press **Open minifigure**.
2. Read **Consists of**. It lists every part of the figure with its colour and, on each line, a link such as **Found in 12 sets**.

If you also want to keep the figure, **Add to stock** on the same card files it as loose stock. You do not need that for this tutorial.

## 3. Follow a part to the sets that hold it

1. On one line of **Consists of**, select the part's name or the **Found in … sets** link. The part page opens at the **Found in** module, set to that colour.
2. Read the colour chip and the words *this colour only*. The module answers for that colour and no other.
3. Read the two headline lines. The first says how many sets and minifigures hold the part. The second says how many items there are and how many of them you own.
4. Look at the groups below. **Owned** comes first: your own copies and loose figures, each with its storage place or **Not stored**. **Catalogue** follows, newest first, with theme and year.
5. Read the quantity on the right of each row, for example ×2. It is how many of this part that set or figure holds, not how many you own.

A rare printed part holds few sets, so its list is short. A common plate holds hundreds, and then the next step helps.

## 4. Narrow the list

1. Type a name, number or theme under **Search** and press **Apply**.
2. Open **Filters** and set **Type** to **Sets** or **Minifigures**. A chip shows the filter; remove it to see all types again.
3. Change **Sort** to **Newest first**, **Oldest first**, **Number** or **Name**, and press **Apply**.
4. Use the page numbers, **Previous** and **Next** to move through the results, 15 at a time.

If the module says **Catalogue list not loaded**, only your owned items appear. Follow **Catalogues & keys** to load the catalogue, then come back.

## 5. Open the set

1. Select the name of a row. An owned row opens your copy. A catalogue row opens the set's page.
2. On a set you do not own and want, press **Add to wishlist** on its row. **On wishlist** means it is already wanted.

You now know which sets the piece may come from and which of them you own. To check the piece against a copy, open the copy and look at its part list.

## 6. Try it with a loose part

A single part works the same way, with one difference. A photo does not show the colour, so Brick Work lists your colours of that part instead.

1. Open **What is this?** again and choose **Part** under **Recognise**.
2. Take or choose the photo and read the best match.
3. Press **Open part**. Choose a colour if the page asks for one.
4. Find **Found in**, below **Used in sets**, and read it as in step 3.

**Used in sets** describes only your own collection. **Found in** also lists sets you do not own, so it helps with a part you have never filed.
