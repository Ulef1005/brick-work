Which parts is my used LEGO® set missing?

Work through the set's parts list one line at a time and write down every difference. At the end you have an exact list of what is missing.

If you bought the set new, ask the LEGO Group first: its replacement parts page is the route for missing pieces from a new set. For a used or inherited set, you need its parts list and some patience.

The answer

  1. 1
    Find the set's parts listIt names each part, its colour and how many the set needs.
  2. 2
    Spread the parts outRoughly by colour or by type, so you can find each one fast.
  3. 3
    Go line by lineCount what you find and write down the difference. A line with 4 needed and 3 found is 1 missing.
  4. 4
    Leave the spares asideMany sets come with a few spare parts, and they are not part of the build.
  5. 5
    Check the minifigures lastAfter the parts, as whole figures.
  6. 6
    Keep the list of gapsIt is your shopping list, and it tells you whether the set is worth finishing.

How Brick Work does it

Set Inspector walks you through one copy of a set, part line by part line, and saves every answer as you go.

  • Pick the copy and an order to work in: the inventory order, by colour, largest first or by category.
  • For each line, count with − and + and confirm what you found. The difference is recorded as missing. Missing marks the whole line, Skip for later leaves it open, and Undo takes back the last answer.
  • The part's name is filled with its own colour, so you can match it against the pile.
  • Minifigures come after the parts. Spare parts are optional and never counted as missing.
  • The top of the page shows how far you are, such as "142 of 375 parts". Close the page and resume any time.
  • At the end you get the list of everything still missing. Part Runner, Matchmaker and the BrickLink wanted-list export all work from it.

The copy needs its part list from the catalogue that Brick Work loads.
